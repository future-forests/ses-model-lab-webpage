B2 — Principles of DM
Responsibility for future forests (RespFor)
Stefan Baumgärtner, Marvin Daab
PhD — Philipp Stark
Stakeholder analysis · Semi-structured in-depth interviews · Qualitative content analysis (MAXQDA)
  • Applies a general and encompassing concept of causal, normative and virtuous responsibility to forest SES
  • Identifies relevant actors and their interrelations through stakeholder analysis; describes each stakeholder's generic role, relations and specific personal identities
  • Elicits stakeholders' specifications of normative responsibility as a six-place predicate (subject, object, respect, extent, authority, justification)
  • Studies whether decision-makers actually live up to self-reported and externally-perceived responsibilities; tests hypothesis of systematic gaps due to differing and unclear notions

B3 — Practices of DM
Knowledge barriers and knowledge practices in everyday forest management (KnowBPs)
Sarah May, Stephanie Bethmann
PhD — Enno Link
Grounded theory · Ethnographic analysis of work · Participant observation · Narrative interviews
  • Follows forest owners, managers and administrators in daily work to understand how tacit, incorporated knowledge is challenged in crisis contexts
  • Uses bark beetle infestation as focal field; draws on monitoring data on forest stands, beetle infestations and clearings to select and compare actors across study regions
  • Traces knowledge barriers hypothesised to run along boundaries of communication, relationship, evidence, ownership, profession, gender and power
  • Pays special attention to field-immanent concepts of affect and emotion: risk, fear, hope, trust, responsibilities
B3 — Practices of DM
Imagining future forests: DM practices in the introduction of contested tree species
Michaela Haug, Axel Albrecht, Stephanie Bethmann
PhD — Sandra Tikale
Ethnographic fieldwork · Participant observation · Informal and semi-structured interviews · Participatory methods · More-than-human approach
  • Traces contested tree species (Red oak, Douglas fir, Late-flowering black cherry, Hungarian oak, Tree hazel, Oriental beech) across all cluster study regions
  • Examines how ecological properties, cultural norms, future imaginaries and institutional requirements interact in decisions about introduction, rejection or classification
  • Applies a more-than-human approach, recognising how certain tree characteristics make certain measures necessary while ruling out others
