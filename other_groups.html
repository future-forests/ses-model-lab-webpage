B1 — Evidentiary Bases of DM
Epistemic authority and disputes under deep uncertainty (EpistAD)
Michael Pregernig
PhD — Adam Smoleń
Qualitative case study design · Interpretive meta-analyses · Scoping and synthesising reviews · Interviews and participant observations · Discourse and frame analysis · Artifact analysis
  • What counts as credible knowledge and how do standards of facticity in SES research change under deep uncertainty?
  • Which modes of knowledge production are scientifically justified and socially accepted under fundamental social ambiguity?

B2 — Principles of DM
Legitimacy and justice as decision-making principles for novel SES
Hartmut Fünfgeld, Daniela Kleinschmit
PostDoc — Jakob Kramer
Integrative literature review · Document analysis · Semi-structured key informant interviews · Focus groups (potentially scenario-based)
  • Traces intra- and intergenerational justice claims through a trivalent framework: distributional, procedural and recognition justice
  • Analyses input, throughput and output legitimacy of political processes shaping future forests under uncertainty
  • Empirical application at three governance levels: Black Forest regional, German national, EU level
  • Reveals power relations, diverse ways of understanding, and inconsistencies in legality and legitimacy
B2 — Principles of DM
Responsibility for future forests (RespFor)
Stefan Baumgärtner, Marvin Daab
PhD — Philipp Stark
Stakeholder analysis · Semi-structured in-depth interviews · Qualitative content analysis (MAXQDA)
  • Applies a general and encompassing concept of causal, normative and virtuous responsibility to forest SES
  • Identifies relevant actors and their interrelations through stakeholder analysis; describes each stakeholder's generic role, relations and specific personal identities
  • Elicits stakeholders' specifications of normative responsibility as a six-place predicate (subject, object, respect, extent, authority, justification)
  • Studies whether decision-makers actually live up to self-reported and externally-perceived responsibilities; tests hypothesis of systematic gaps due to differing and unclear notions

B3 — Practices of DM
Knowledge barriers and knowledge practices in everyday forest management (KnowBPs)
Sarah May, Stephanie Bethmann
PhD — Enno Link
Grounded theory · Ethnographic analysis of work · Participant observation · Narrative interviews
  • Follows forest owners, managers and administrators in daily work to understand how tacit, incorporated knowledge is challenged in crisis contexts
  • Uses bark beetle infestation as focal field; draws on monitoring data on forest stands, beetle infestations and clearings to select and compare actors across study regions
  • Traces knowledge barriers hypothesised to run along boundaries of communication, relationship, evidence, ownership, profession, gender and power
  • Pays special attention to field-immanent concepts of affect and emotion: risk, fear, hope, trust, responsibilities
B3 — Practices of DM
Imagining future forests: DM practices in the introduction of contested tree species
Michaela Haug, Axel Albrecht, Stephanie Bethmann
PhD — Sandra Tikale
Ethnographic fieldwork · Participant observation · Informal and semi-structured interviews · Participatory methods · More-than-human approach
  • Traces contested tree species (Red oak, Douglas fir, Late-flowering black cherry, Hungarian oak, Tree hazel, Oriental beech) across all cluster study regions
  • Examines how ecological properties, cultural norms, future imaginaries and institutional requirements interact in decisions about introduction, rejection or classification
  • Applies a more-than-human approach, recognising how certain tree characteristics make certain measures necessary while ruling out others
  • Investigates how decisions mirror broader societal negotiations over environmental futures and continuities in human–forest relations
B3 — Practices of DM
Diversity in human–nature relationships as adaptation potential for disputed forest futures (HuNaRe)
Stephanie Bethmann
PhD — Wiebke Hebermehl
Qualitative methods · Arts-based and performative research · Collaborative photography · Reflexive grounded theory · Comparative ethnography
  • Studies citizen initiatives, cooperatives, and protest camps in Baden-Württemberg that challenge forestry decision-making.
  • Analyses how diverse human–nature relationships shape decision-making, foresters’ roles, and resilience, including risks and opportunities for adaptation.
  • Links local practices to global movements such as rights of nature, care, planetary health, and Indigenous ontologies.
  • Develops social change scenarios and supports participatory modelling with conceptual and empirical inputs.
  • Facilitates transdisciplinary dialogue and collective learning.
  • Examines how forestry institutions respond as established decision-making frameworks lose effectiveness.

C1 — SES Theory & Concepts
Integrating laws and legal institutions into SES theory and its application to future forests
Cathrin Zengerling
PhD — Leonie Buß
Systematic literature review · Overview of SES/CAS frameworks · Doctrinal legal analysis · Interdisciplinary co-design with C1 and B2
  • Creates systematic overview of current state of research on SES and law; assesses how current SES frameworks account for the role of law and legal institutions
  • Develops options for including law and legal institutions into the FuFo SES framework, considering spaces of integration and pluralism
  • Identifies what knowledge of the legal system is needed to inform scenario development, modelling, governance experiments and A&TPs in RA C
