Future Forests Excellence Cluster · Universität Freiburg · SES-ModelLab

Modeling and research cooperation groups

Group 1
Forest growth and disturbance dynamics
Goal
Project how forests grow, die, and recover under climate stress and disturbance.
What is studied
Forest growth, disturbance, and mortality Tree mortality and cover dynamics from remote sensing Stand structure and trait variation along disturbance gradients Resilience and recovery after drought or disturbance How density management affects tree fitness Whether models represent forest dynamics well enough for decisions
Who fits here
You work on how forests grow, die, or recover — whether through field measurements, remote sensing, simulation, or model evaluation. You produce or need data on forest structure, mortality, traits, or growth at stand or landscape scale. You also fit here if you care about whether forest models are well-founded and fit for decision-making: which processes matter, how well they are represented, and what evidence would be needed to trust a model's projections.
Group produces
  • Forest growth simulations
  • Mortality and disturbance maps
  • Tree trait and structural diversity data
  • Growth and resilience indices
  • Model evaluation outputs
Group 2
Drought, hydrology, and forest ecosystem responses
Goal
Understand how drought, heat stress, and shifting water availability affect forest carbon and water fluxes — and project how these change under future climate and management scenarios.
What is studied
How soil moisture deficit and atmospheric drought drive forest carbon uptake and water use Vegetation stress responses using plant traits instead of generic categories Current-state forest vulnerability across Baden-Württemberg Soil water flow from rainfall to streamflow Groundwater recharge under different forest covers How forest composition change propagates through catchment hydrology
Who fits here
You work on how forests respond to water stress — at the leaf, stand, catchment, or regional scale. You study carbon and water fluxes under drought or heat, model soil moisture, runoff, or groundwater, or connect forest physiology to hydrological outcomes. You also fit here if you produce the climate and hydroclimatic forcing data that makes these projections possible — regional climate scenarios, downscaled reanalysis, or extreme event storylines — or if your question is which management interventions can actually reduce drought and heat impacts on forests.
Group produces
  • Forest vulnerability maps for Baden-Württemberg
  • Improved trait-based vegetation stress functions
  • Updated ecosystem model for drought responses
  • Spatially explicit hydrology datasets with uncertainty estimates
Group 3
Sub-canopy microclimate modelling
Goal
Map the climate conditions experienced inside forests at the resolution and spatial extent needed by biodiversity, drought, and ecosystem service research across FuFo.
What is studied
Temperature, humidity, and radiation below the forest canopy — 30-year hindcast Deadwood distribution and its climatic drivers Groundwater nitrate concentrations linked to forest dieback Acoustic environments inside forests
Who fits here
You work with sensor network data or remote sensing to characterise conditions inside forests. You build or use data-driven models trained on environmental observations. Your research depends on fine-scale temperature, humidity, light, or acoustic conditions below the canopy — or on understanding how forest structure and composition mediate those conditions for biodiversity, people, or ecosystem function.
Group produces
  • High-resolution sub-canopy climate datasets
  • Deadwood forecasting maps
  • Groundwater nitrate products
Group 4
Participatory landscape modelling
Goal
Connect stakeholder knowledge, governance frameworks, and management objectives to spatially explicit forest simulations — so that scenarios are grounded in what actors actually value, decide, and are legally permitted to do.
What is studied
Forest resilience through statistical early warning indicators Landscape-scale forest transformation scenarios co-designed with stakeholders Stakeholder decision-making processes and governance constraints Biodiversity trade-offs from renewables and infrastructure Environmental impacts of forest-related land-use decisions
Who fits here
You work on what people think, decide, or argue about forests — through interviews, ethnography, legal analysis, or governance research. You study stakeholder values, responsibility, legitimacy, or decision-making under uncertainty. You develop governance frameworks, legal scenarios, or social change scenarios that shape what future forests are allowed or expected to become. You also fit here if you connect this qualitative knowledge to quantitative landscape models, or if you use model outputs to understand whether governance choices are realistic, legitimate, or fair.
Group produces
  • Stakeholder knowledge on forest values and decision-making
  • Legal scenarios and governance frameworks
  • Spatially explicit landscape simulations with ecosystem service indicators
Group 5
Forest bioeconomy modelling
Goal
Trace where harvested wood ends up and what this means for climate, markets, and forest management — answering when and under what conditions leaving wood in the forest is preferable to harvesting it.
What is studied
The fate of carbon in harvested wood across product types and time horizons Stocks and flows of ecosystem services through market networks Systemic dynamics of the forest bioeconomy under different use and governance scenarios Trend interactions shaping forest social-ecological futures Spatial distribution of ecosystem services and tools for their allocation
Who fits here
You work on the economic, material, or market dimensions of forest management — tracing timber, carbon, ecosystem services, or wood products through supply chains and governance systems. You model how forest decisions translate into economic or climate outcomes, or develop tools for spatial planning, governance incentives, and payment schemes for ecosystem services. You also fit here if you work on the principles and trade-offs behind those decisions — robustness, responsibility, and legitimacy in the face of deep uncertainty about future markets, climate, and values.
Group produces
  • Carbon time series of harvested wood across product types and technology scenarios
  • Trend interaction maps for social-ecological and forestry futures
  • Ecosystem service maps and quantification tools
