Group 1 · joint session
Forest growth, disturbance, hydrology & sub-canopy microclimate
Goal
Project how forests grow, die, and recover under climate stress, drought, and disturbance — and characterise the sub-canopy conditions that mediate those responses. Work across canopy structure, water fluxes, and microclimate at scales from stand to landscape.
What is studied
Forest growth, disturbance, and mortality dynamics Tree mortality and cover change from remote sensing Stand structure, trait variation, and resilience along disturbance and management gradients Soil moisture and atmospheric drought effects on carbon and water fluxes Trait-based vegetation stress responses Soil water flow, runoff, and groundwater recharge under different forest covers Temperature, humidity, radiation, and acoustic conditions below the canopy Deadwood distribution and groundwater nitrate as forest-condition indicators Whether and how well models represent these processes for decision-relevant projections
Who fits here
You work on how forests grow, die, recover, or experience their internal climate — through field measurements, sensor networks, remote sensing, simulation, or model evaluation. You produce or need data on forest structure, mortality, traits, growth, water fluxes, or sub-canopy conditions at stand, catchment, or landscape scale. You also fit here if you generate climate and hydroclimatic forcing data, or if your question is whether current models are well-founded enough to inform management and policy.
Group produces
  • Forest growth and disturbance simulations
  • Mortality, disturbance, and cover-change maps
  • Trait, structural, and resilience datasets
  • Forest vulnerability maps for Baden-Württemberg
  • Improved trait-based vegetation stress functions
  • Updated ecosystem model for drought responses
  • Spatially explicit hydrology datasets with uncertainty
  • High-resolution sub-canopy climate datasets
  • Deadwood and groundwater nitrate products
  • Model evaluation outputs

Group 2
Participatory landscape modelling
Goal
Connect stakeholder knowledge, governance frameworks, and management objectives to spatially explicit forest simulations — so that scenarios are grounded in what actors actually value, decide, and are legally permitted to do.
What is studied
Forest resilience through statistical early warning indicators Landscape-scale forest transformation scenarios co-designed with stakeholders Stakeholder decision-making processes and governance constraints Biodiversity trade-offs from renewables and infrastructure Environmental impacts of forest-related land-use decisions
Who fits here
You work on what people think, decide, or argue about forests — through interviews, ethnography, legal analysis, or governance research. You study stakeholder values, responsibility, legitimacy, or decision-making under uncertainty. You develop governance frameworks, legal scenarios, or social change scenarios that shape what future forests are allowed or expected to become. You also fit here if you connect this qualitative knowledge to quantitative landscape models, or if you use model outputs to understand whether governance choices are realistic, legitimate, or fair.
Group produces
  • Stakeholder knowledge on forest values and decision-making
  • Legal scenarios and governance frameworks
  • Spatially explicit landscape simulations with ecosystem service indicators

Group 3
Social metabolism/industrial ecology of wood and wood products
Goal
Trace where harvested wood ends up and what this means for climate, markets, and forest management — answering when and under what conditions leaving wood in the forest is preferable to harvesting it.
What is studied
The fate of carbon in harvested wood across product types and time horizons Stocks and flows of ecosystem services through market networks Systemic dynamics of the forest bioeconomy under different use and governance scenarios Trend interactions shaping forest social-ecological futures Spatial distribution of ecosystem services and tools for their allocation
Who fits here
You work on the economic, material, or market dimensions of forest management — tracing timber, carbon, ecosystem services, or wood products through supply chains and governance systems. You model how forest decisions translate into economic or climate outcomes, or develop tools for spatial planning, governance incentives, and payment schemes for ecosystem services. You also fit here if you work on the principles and trade-offs behind those decisions — robustness, responsibility, and legitimacy in the face of deep uncertainty about future markets, climate, and values.
Group produces
  • Carbon time series of harvested wood across product types and technology scenarios
  • Trend interaction maps for social-ecological and forestry futures
  • Ecosystem service maps and quantification tools
