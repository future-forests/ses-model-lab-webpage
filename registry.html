Evidence Process Model
Matching purpose, model and evidence for forest management
Prof. Dr. Carsten Dormann · Yannek Käber · Prof. Dr. Michael Pregernig

  • Contributes code improvements to open-source forest growth models for decision support under climate change (with C3 and SES ModelLab)
Research objectives
  1. Catalogue the evidentiary base of processes in existing open-source forest models
  2. Match temperate forest models to questions decision makers seek to address
  3. Improve a FuFo forest-growth model in respects that fall short of requirements for decision making under uncertainty

Wood Cascade Modelling
A&TPs for wood use and cascading of wood products
Prof. Dr. Stefan PauliukWood Cascade and Carbon Accounting (A&TPs)
Also: Dr. Simon Schulte · Prof. Dr. Heiner Schanz · Jun.-Prof. Dr. Ana Stritih · Prof. Dr. Marc Hanewinkel · Prof. Dr. Maja Schlüter
Methods: ModellingRECC (stock-flow consistent process model)
  • Develops a modelling and stakeholder engagement framework for identifying and evaluating A&TPs for wood use and cascading
  • Defines A&TPs as sequences of decisions by agents in the wood use system that meet given objectives and respect given principles of responsibility and legitimacy
  • Three steps: mapping the option space via scenarios; combining processing data and stakeholder input to identify pathways; assessing achievability under uncertainty
Research objectives
  1. Translate B decision-making principles into agent descriptions and modelling principles for wood industry A&TPs
  2. Quantify circular economy potential and climate benefit of wood use and cascading in the study region
  3. Assess robustness of wood processing industries under disruptive events and technologies
  4. Develop pathways to novel SES-informed regime of wood processing and use

Knowledge Graph for Forest Data (WWW 4.0)

Project partners: Ministry for Nutrition, Rural Affairs and Consumer Protection of Baden-Württemberg (MLR), Forestry Directorate in Freiburg (Forstdirektion Freiburg), Forest Research Institute Baden-Württemberg (FVA), Department of Computer Science and Institute of Sociology at the University of Freiburg
Knowledge graphSemantic data integration
Method: Integrating heterogeneous data formats into a shared semantic structure. In this way, data are not only connected across different sources and formats, but are also organized according to the explicit relationships between entities, enabling context-sensitive querying and analysis.
  • Forest site mapping (Standortskartierung): soil properties, water balance, topology
  • 10-year forest management planning (Forsteinrichtung)
  • Tree species suitability assessments and climate-informed species forecasts (FVA model outputs)
  • Digital terrain model
  • Planting numbers and spacing guidelines
  • Silviculture manual (WET)
  • Nature conservation designations and certification status
  • Subsidy programme data
  • Operational forest management data
Output: The knowledge graph can serve both as a basis for modelling and simulation approaches, and as an environment for advanced analyses within the graph itself, such as identifying patterns, dependencies, and complex relationships between entities.
